Terms of Service

The ground rules for using this site and for working with HarshaForge, in plain language.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

The short version. Sending an enquiry commits neither of us to anything. A project starts only when you accept a written proposal. You own the custom work we build for you once it is paid for, and we are honest about what we can and cannot promise.

1. About these terms

These terms apply to this website and to any work HarshaForge Digital Solutions ("HarshaForge", "we", "us") does for you ("you", "the client"). If we agree a written proposal with you, it forms part of these terms, and where the two differ, the proposal applies to that project.

2. Using this website

You may use the site to learn about our services and to contact us. You agree not to:

  • send spam, or submit false or misleading information through the form,
  • try to disrupt, probe or gain unauthorised access to the site or its server, or
  • copy our text, design or code to present as your own.

The site's content, design and code belong to HarshaForge unless stated otherwise. Our Privacy Policy explains how we handle what you send us.

3. Enquiries and consultations

Sending an enquiry or booking a consultation is free and creates no obligation for either side. Nothing said before a written proposal is accepted is a binding quote or a promise of delivery. We reply by email, usually within two working days, and we will tell you plainly if your project is not a good fit for us.

4. Project engagements

A project begins when you accept our written proposal. The proposal sets out:

  • what we will build, and what is not included,
  • the price, or the rate if the work is hourly or ongoing,
  • the payment schedule, and
  • the expected timeline and the main milestones.

Changes. If you want something added or changed after the scope is agreed, we will tell you what it means for cost and timing before we start on it.

What we need from you. Timelines depend on you giving us access, information, feedback and decisions when we ask for them. Delays on your side may move the delivery date.

Payment. You agree to pay invoices by the dates stated in the proposal. Taxes are added where they apply. We may pause work on a project while an invoice is overdue.

5. Who owns the work

  • Custom work for you. Once you have paid in full, you own the custom software, designs and documents we build specifically for you.
  • Our general tools. We keep ownership of pre-existing code, templates and methods we use across projects. You receive a permanent right to use them as part of your system.
  • Third-party components. Open-source libraries and paid services stay under their own licences, which we will point out when they matter.
  • Showing our work. We will not name you or show your system in our portfolio without your written permission.

6. Confidentiality

We treat your business information, data and access credentials as confidential. We use them only to do the work, and we do not share them except with your permission or where the law requires. This continues after the project ends.

7. Support after launch

Any fixes, support window or ongoing partnership will be described in your proposal. Where no support is agreed, later changes and fixes are quoted separately. We hand systems over documented, so you are never locked in to depending on us.

8. What we promise, and what we do not

We will do the work with reasonable skill and care, and we will be honest about progress and problems. We cannot guarantee a specific amount of time or money saved. Any figures we give are estimates based on what you tell us, not promises.

To the extent the law allows, we are not liable for indirect or consequential losses, such as lost profit or lost business. Our total liability for any claim connected to a project is limited to the fees you paid us for that project. Nothing in these terms limits liability that cannot lawfully be limited.

9. Third-party services

Some systems rely on services we do not control, such as hosting, email delivery or payment providers. We choose them with care, but we are not responsible for their outages, pricing changes or terms.

10. Ending an engagement

Either of us can end a project by written notice. You pay for work completed up to that point, and for any costs we have already committed on your behalf. We will hand over the work completed and paid for, together with the documentation we have so far.

11. Governing law

These terms are governed by the laws of India. If a disagreement comes up, we will first try to settle it by talking. Failing that, the courts of India have jurisdiction.

12. Changes to these terms

We may update these terms from time to time. The date at the top shows the latest version. Changes do not alter the terms of a proposal you have already accepted.
