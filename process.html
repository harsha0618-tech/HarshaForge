Process

How a project runs, from first message to launch

Step-by-step detail

1

Discover

We talk through the problem, not the technology — your current workflow, who uses it, and what a good outcome actually looks like. No obligation, no jargon.

What happens

A conversation about what's slowing you down, how the current process works, and whether custom software is a sensible fit at all.

What you provide

An honest description of the problem, examples of the current process — spreadsheets, forms, screenshots — and who will use the system.

What you get

A written summary of the problem, and a straight answer on whether it's worth building — even if that answer is "not yet."

2

Design

We sketch the plan — screens, data, and workflow — before a line of code is written, so you can see the shape of the system and ask for changes while they're still cheap to make.

What happens

We turn the discovery conversation into a simple plan: the screens, the data it needs to track, and how it fits your workflow.

What you provide

Feedback on the plan, plus any must-haves — compliance rules, specific fields, or existing tools it needs to work alongside.

What you get

A plan you can actually review, and a clear scope and estimate before any build work begins.

3

Build

Development happens in stages, with regular check-ins rather than disappearing for weeks and reappearing with one big reveal at the end.

What happens

The system gets built piece by piece, in the order that lets you see and use real parts of it earliest.

What you provide

Timely answers when something needs a decision, and access to any accounts or data the system needs to connect to.

What you get

Visibility into progress as it happens, and the chance to redirect early if something doesn't match how your team actually works.

4

Test

We test against real use cases — not just the easy path, but busy days, odd data, and the mistakes someone's realistically going to make.

What happens

We run the system through ordinary use and the awkward edge cases — not just the scenario that always works in a demo.

What you provide

Real, or realistic, data to test with, and a short list of people willing to try it before launch.

What you get

A system that's been checked against your actual workflow, with issues caught before your customers ever see them.

5

Launch

We move the system live, bring over any existing data that needs to come with it, and make sure the handover is clean rather than rushed.

What happens

The system goes live. If you're moving from spreadsheets or an old tool, existing records are migrated across.

What you provide

Sign-off that it's ready, and access to whatever hosting or domain details launch requires.

What you get

A live system, plus plain-language documentation so your team isn't dependent on memory — or on us — to use it.

6

Support

Launch isn't the end of the conversation. We stay reachable for questions, fixes, and small adjustments once you're actually using the system day to day.

What happens

Real use surfaces things a plan never could. We fix what's broken and adjust what's awkward.

What you provide

A heads-up on what's working and what isn't, once your team has had a chance to actually use it.

What you get

A direct line to the person who built it — not a ticket number and a queue.

How long this takes

We won't quote a generic timeline, because it depends on the project — a small automation can move through all six steps in days; a full management system takes longer. You'll get a specific estimate at the end of Design, once the actual scope is clear, not a guess before we've talked.
